Where does one panel help?

The shelter and passenger positions are fictional. The dated presets use approximate Hong Kong sun angles for 23 November 2026 at 22.3° N, 114.2° E. The original 40° scenario uses assumed angles. All points are evaluated at ground level.

Dashed outline = roof or added panel. Green patch = projected shade. Filled circle = shaded queue point. Hollow circle = exposed point. Square = seat reference point.

Weighted total = shaded queue points + the selected weight if the seat point is shaded. Weight expresses a design preference. The two panels have identical dimensions. Shadow length equals roof height divided by the tangent of the solar elevation. Shadows from buildings and trees are omitted. North is up and east is right on this plan.

Can the image tell sun from shade?

A nearest-centroid model learns two average colours from labelled pixels on one pavement crop. The green overlay marks pixels it assigns to shade. Try the dark strip to see where it makes a mistake.

Images stay on this device. Crop to empty pavement before opening. New images need examples of both classes. On a phone, tap a sample in the left image.
